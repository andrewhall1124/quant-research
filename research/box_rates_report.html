What rate do options actually discount at?

A box spread is a synthetic zero-coupon bond built from four option quotes. Its yield is the rate the options market is really financing at — and across 2025 it sat 16bp above the Treasury curve, not on top of SOFR.

The obvious way to ask this question is to compare option-implied rates to SOFR, since SOFR is the rate everyone quotes. That comparison is available below, and it looks like a clean match — mean box rate 4.218% against mean SOFR 4.226%, a median gap of under 3bp.

That match is mostly a coincidence of a flat year. The boxes that survive measurement are 6–12 months out, and SOFR is an overnight rate; holding them side by side conflates the level of rates with the slope of the curve. Once each box is compared to a Treasury yield at its own maturity, correlation rises from 0.46 to 0.77 and a persistent, economically sensible spread appears.

Why a box, and not put-call parity

The instrument choice does most of the work here.

For two strikes sharing an expiration, put-call parity applied twice and differenced gives a position whose payoff is a known constant. Everything about the underlying cancels:

Box spread (C1 − P1) − (C2 − P2) = (K2 − K1) · e−rT   ⟹   r = ln( (K₂−K₁) / cost ) / T

The spot level drops out, and with it the entire dividend stream. A box therefore needs no dividend assumption and no underlying price — only four option quotes. That matters practically, because dividend and corporate-action data is exactly what this dataset lacks.

Single-strike put-call parity has no such immunity: it depends on spot and silently absorbs dividends into the rate. Run on the same index, it produces 3.530% against the box's 4.218% — a 65bp gap that is the market's dividend expectation showing up as a rate error. The S&P yielded roughly 1.2–1.3% in 2025, so parity recovers only about half of it; the rest is timing mismatch between the 16:00 index print and the option snapshot.

Tracking through the year

Daily medians. The box follows the Treasury curve's shape; SOFR steps with policy instead.

The last two months are the most informative stretch. SOFR falls hard as the Fed cuts — 4.21% in October to 3.81% in December — while the box rate declines only modestly, from 4.08% to 4.02%. A box spanning 6–12 months prices the average expected rate over that window, so it cannot follow an overnight rate down step for step. The apparent widening to +21bp against SOFR in December is the curve steepening, not the options repricing.

The term structure is real

Longer boxes price lower — the market pricing cuts it expected to happen.

That downward slope is the market pricing the easing cycle, and it survives in the raw unfiltered data too (4.46% → 4.26% → 3.97%), so it isn't an artifact of the quality screen.

The central result

Box rates sit above the Treasury curve by a median 16bp and converge toward it at longer maturities. That sign is what theory predicts: a box is unsecured synthetic financing, while Treasuries carry a collateral and safety premium that pushes their yield down. The options market discounts at something closer to a funding rate than to a risk-free rate.

What the measurement can and cannot see

Two limits are structural, and both bound how far this can be pushed.

Short maturities are unmeasurable

Quote noise enters the rate divided by width × T, so as T shrinks the error explodes. Short-dated boxes are abundant but useless — and this is a property of the instrument, not of the filter:

Do not read equity boxes as rates

Single-stock boxes imply ~1.6%, roughly 260bp below the true financing rate. The short legs carry early-exercise value that the parity identity does not account for, so the box costs more than its discounted payoff and the solved rate is biased toward zero. Cash-settled index options are the only clean instrument here.

Method

  • Universe. SPX and SPXW EOD chains, 2025, 5.9M quoted rows over 258 dates, yielding 3.48M synthetic forwards.
  • Prices. Bid/ask midpoints; both legs must be two-sided with ask > bid > 0.
  • Strike selection. Restricted to ±15% moneyness, then the k-th lowest strike paired with the k-th highest. Deep-ITM legs are excluded deliberately — their quotes are wide and stale.
  • Quality screen. Boxes are kept only when quote noise maps to under 100bp of rate uncertainty, i.e. spread / (width × T) < 0.01. 5,393 of 56,731 survive.
  • Benchmarks. SOFR from ThetaData; Treasury curve linearly interpolated in maturity from the CBOE 13-week, 5-, 10- and 30-year yield indices.

Caveats worth carrying forward

The Treasury curve is interpolated from only four points, and its short end (13 weeks) sits well inside the 6–12 month region where most boxes live — so the term-matched benchmark is itself approximate. EOD midpoints are not executable prices. And 49 of 250 trading days produce no measurable box at all, so the daily series is not a continuous panel.
